How to Claim Your ChatGPT Business Profile (Dubai Guide)

OpenAI opened its Business Hub on 10 November. Here's how to claim and verify your ChatGPT profile from the UAE, what the new Insights tab really shows, and what it won't do for your recommendations.

Yes, you can claim it now. What OpenAI actually switched on this week

On Tuesday 10 November, OpenAI opened something it calls Businesses in ChatGPT. The front door is the Business Hub, a dashboard where you search for your company, prove you own it and fill in the details ChatGPT should know about you. It's in beta, and for now it's free.

Three pieces came out together:

  • Business profiles. Claim, verify, then add your products, services and company details.
  • Insights. An analytics tab showing how often ChatGPT mentions you and sends people to your site.
  • Business agents. A separately labelled chat that speaks for your brand when someone wants to explore a product. United States only, and only for e-commerce and online services businesses so far.

Benji Shomair, OpenAI's VP of monetisation, described the business agent to Digiday as "the organic experience". The paid version, called a sponsored agent, has been in testing since September. Same agent, same conversation, one of them you pay for.

If that sounds a lot like Google Business Profile meets Google Ads, that's because it is. TechCrunch ran the headline that ChatGPT just became "a little bit more like Google". We'd agree.

Why Dubai businesses should care more than most

Two reasons, and neither is hype.

First, people here already ask AI for recommendations. A Dubai Press Club and Deloitte Middle East report from September put organisational AI adoption in the UAE at 75%, the highest in the Arab region. We see the consumer side of that every week. Someone looking for a dermatologist in Jumeirah or a fit-out company in Al Quoz is increasingly typing the whole question into ChatGPT rather than scrolling ten blue links.

Second, the local tooling is catching up fast. Etisalat launched an AI Marketing Hub for small businesses on 13 November, three days after OpenAI's announcement. When the telco and the AI lab both decide SMEs are the market, assume your competitors will be inside these dashboards by the new year.

The businesses that claim early get a clean baseline. The ones that wait will be reading someone else's numbers.

How to claim your ChatGPT business profile

Budget about twenty minutes. Here's the order we'd do it in.

  1. Sign in to the Business Hub with the ChatGPT account you want to own the listing. Use a company account, not a personal one that leaves when a staff member does.
  2. Search for your business. OpenAI may already have a listing for you. Its policy says it can create and show one whether you claim it or not, so don't be surprised if it's there.
  3. Verify. You can prove ownership through a business phone number, an email on your domain, the website domain itself, or a Shopify account. Domain verification is the strongest, so pick that if you can.
  4. Fill in the details properly. Trading name exactly as it appears on your licence and your Google Business Profile. Address. Opening hours. Service areas. A short, factual description. No slogans.
  5. Add products or services. If you sell online, connect the product feed. If you're a service business, list the services in the plain words people actually search for.
  6. Open Insights and screenshot it. That's your day-one baseline. Check it weekly, not daily.

One thing to say out loud. OpenAI states that claiming your business "doesn't guarantee increased visibility or give you direct control over ChatGPT's independent recommendations". Treat the profile as hygiene, like a correct Google listing. It won't win you the recommendation on its own.

What the Insights tab shows, and what it hides

The analytics are the genuinely new part, and they're also where expectations need managing.

You get four things: mentions, views, website clicks and discovery topics. Discovery topics are the themes of conversations in which ChatGPT cited your site. That's useful. If a Business Bay café finds it's being cited for "quiet places to work" and not for "best brunch", that's a content brief handed to you for free.

Now the limits. A mention, for a local business, means ChatGPT showed a result for your location. For an online business, it means ChatGPT referenced your website. Someone typing your brand name into a prompt does not count. Website clicks are clicks on links, not visits that completed or sales that happened. Small numbers may be hidden for privacy. And you never see the prompts or the chats themselves, only the themes.

An ad executive with early access told Digiday the charts cover the past seven or 28 days. There's no share-of-voice figure and no competitor view yet.

Agencies have been estimating this data for a year with tools like Peec.AI, Otterly.AI and Semrush. Farhad Divecha of Accuracast told Digiday he's keen to compare those estimates with OpenAI's own numbers. So are we. Our guess is the third-party tools stay relevant, because ChatGPT is one assistant among several, and Google's AI Overviews are now reportedly appearing on most searches.

Business agents and sponsored agents: not here yet, but prepare

The branded agent is the piece UAE businesses can't switch on today. OpenAI hasn't shared a timeline for other markets, and Shomair declined to give one.

That's fine. The agent is built by OpenAI from two inputs you control right now: your written instructions and your product feed. You can't bring your own bot. So the preparation is unglamorous. Clean product data. Accurate prices in dirhams. Clear delivery areas. A returns policy that's written down somewhere a machine can read it.

Our honest view on sponsored agents: worth testing for e-commerce brands with healthy margins, a waste for most service businesses in the first year. OpenAI itself says paying for ads doesn't change how often ChatGPT recommends you. The ad buys you a conversation, not a ranking.

Does any of this get you recommended more?

Not directly. Shomair told Digiday that business information doesn't affect how often a business appears in recommendations, though it may help ChatGPT surface you in more relevant conversations.

What actually moves the needle hasn't changed since we built our AI-search readiness checklist for clients. You need to be a source ChatGPT and Google trust enough to cite. In practice that means pages that answer one question completely, consistent business details everywhere (licence, Google, Instagram, your own footer), real reviews with real detail, and structured data so machines don't have to guess what you do.

The profile is the label on the jar. The content is what's inside it.

Questions people ask

Is the ChatGPT Business Hub available in the UAE?

Yes. Business profiles and the Insights analytics are open to businesses worldwide as of 10 November 2026. The branded business agents and the paid sponsored agents are limited to e-commerce and online services businesses in the United States for now, and OpenAI hasn't given a date for other markets.

Does claiming my ChatGPT business profile cost anything?

No. OpenAI says the Business Hub, the profile and the business agents are free for now. Benji Shomair, OpenAI's VP of monetisation, didn't rule out charging later. Sponsored agents, the advertising version of the same conversation, are paid.

How do I verify my business on ChatGPT?

Through a business phone number, an email address on your domain, your website domain, or a Shopify account. Domain verification is the strongest option because it ties the listing to the asset you'll keep longest, and it's harder for a former employee to walk away with.

Can I see what people ask ChatGPT about my business?

No. Insights shows discovery topics, which are the themes of conversations where ChatGPT cited your site, plus mentions, views and website clicks. Individual prompts and chats stay with OpenAI, and small counts may be hidden for privacy. Think of it as a weather report, not a transcript.

Will a claimed profile make ChatGPT recommend my business more often?

OpenAI says no. Business information doesn't change how often you're recommended, though accurate details may help ChatGPT place you in more relevant conversations. Being cited by trusted sources, consistent business details and well-structured pages still do the heavy lifting.
